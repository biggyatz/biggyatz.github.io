What I learned training 200+ finance professionals to use AI

Six lessons from training 200+ finance professionals: automate around legacy systems, keep every number traceable, keep humans signing off.

Biggyat Kumar Pandey
3 November 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: you don't need new software to use AI in finance. Automate around the system you have, start with one repetitive task, keep every number traceable, and keep a qualified person signing off.

Between May and July 2026 I trained more than 200 corporate finance professionals through ACCA Nepal to use AI and Python in their own work. Most had the same starting point: a legacy accounting system nobody was going to replace, spreadsheets around it, and a lot of repetitive work in between. These are the principles I teach.

Key lessons

  • Don't rebuild the system; automate around it. Every accounting system exports something. Start from the export and leave the books untouched.
  • Start with one task everyone hates. Pick something repetitive and measurable, and time it before and after.
  • Use AI where it earns its keep: extracting data from PDFs and statements, flagging reconciliation mismatches, recurring reports, first drafts of commentary and data checks.
  • Keep a human signing off final figures, judgement calls and anything regulatory or client-facing.
  • Make every number traceable from the final report back to the source. If a reviewer can't trace it, it isn't ready.
  • Protect the data. Never paste client or company data into a public AI chatbot.

As I put it in every session: "A model is only worth what the person reading it can act on."

Frequently asked questions

Can AI work with old accounting software?

Yes. Almost every accounting system can export CSV, Excel or PDF reports, and AI and Python workflows can process those exports without replacing or modifying the core system.

Should AI replace human review in finance?

No. AI should prepare and check the work, while a qualified person approves final figures, judgement calls and anything regulatory or client-facing.

Is it safe to use ChatGPT with financial data?

Not with confidential client or company data in a public tool. Use approved tools, keep sensitive data on systems you control, and anonymise samples when experimenting.
