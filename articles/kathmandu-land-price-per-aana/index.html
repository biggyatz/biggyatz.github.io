What land in Kathmandu costs per aana: asking prices from 1,923 listings

Median asking prices per aana for land and houses across 11 districts and 111 localities, and what road access, storeys and size do to the price.

Biggyat Kumar Pandey
3 November 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: across 1,923 listings, the median asking price for land was about Rs 27 lakh per aana in Kathmandu, Rs 23 lakh in Bhaktapur and Rs 17 lakh in Lalitpur. Prime localities like Baluwatar and Dhumbarahi asked Rs 55–60 lakh. Houses asked about Rs 51–67 lakh per aana in the valley because the price includes the building.

Ask five people in Kathmandu what a plot is worth and you'll get five answers, usually based on what a relative sold for years ago. I wanted numbers instead. So I collected 1,923 property listings from 99aana.com (1,742) and Hamrobazaar (181), converted every price and every Nepali land unit into one scale, and built a free price estimator on top of it. This article shares what the data says.

One caveat up front: these are asking prices, not final sale prices. Sellers in Nepal usually ask above what they accept, so treat every figure as an upper reference point. Most listings (1,411) are from 2021, with smaller sets from 2022 to 2026.

Two things stand out. First, a house in the valley asks roughly 2 to 4 times more per aana than an empty plot in the same district (2.2 times in Bhaktapur, 2.4 in Kathmandu, 3.9 in Lalitpur), because you are paying for the building too. Second, land outside the valley is a different market altogether: a typical aana in Chitwan or Rupandehi asks less than a sixth of the Kathmandu figure.

What a typical Kathmandu valley house asks

Across 928 house listings in Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur, the typical (median) house sits on 3.5 aana and asks Rs 2.38 crore. The middle half of listings ask between Rs 1.65 crore and Rs 3.35 crore. Across 794 land listings in the valley, the median asking price is Rs 25 lakh per aana.

What changes the price, holding location fixed

To separate the effect of each feature, I fitted a regression on the logarithm of price that compares like with like: the same locality, property type and size. The estimator uses this model. It found:

  • Road access matters. Doubling the road width in front of a property (say from 10 to 20 feet) goes with about a 7% higher asking price.
  • Each extra storey adds about 23% to a house's asking price, other things equal.
  • Size has diminishing returns. Price grows a little slower than area, so larger plots ask less per aana than small ones in the same area.
  • Listings from 2022 and 2023 asked about 20 to 22% more than comparable 2021 listings. There are too few 2024 to 2026 listings to say whether prices have risen or cooled since.

The model explains about 75% of the variation in (log) asking prices, and its typical error on listings it had not seen is about 24%. That is good enough for a first sense of value, not for a bank valuation. Things it cannot see, like the exact plot shape, title clarity, slope or the view, often decide the final price.

Converting Nepali land units

The hills and the Terai use different systems. Here is the arithmetic the estimator uses:

  • Ropani system (hills, valley): 1 ropani = 16 aana = 5,476 sq ft. 1 aana = 4 paisa = 342.25 sq ft. 1 paisa = 4 dam.
  • Bigha system (Terai): 1 bigha = 20 kattha = 72,900 sq ft. 1 kattha = 20 dhur = 3,645 sq ft, or about 10.65 aana. 1 dhur = 182.25 sq ft.

So "0-4-0-0" (ropani-aana-paisa-dam) means 4 aana, or 1,369 sq ft, and a 5 kattha Terai plot is about 53 aana.

How to use these numbers

  1. Start from the locality median, not the district median. Location within the valley swings prices more than anything else.
  2. Adjust for road width and, for houses, the number of storeys.
  3. Remember that asking prices are a ceiling. Negotiate from comparable listings, not from the first number you hear.
  4. For a specific property, try the estimator: enter the area in ropani-aana or bigha-kattha-dhur and it shows a likely range and similar listings.

The data was collected only from public listing pages, following each site's robots.txt rules, and no personal data such as phone numbers was kept.

Frequently asked questions

How much is one aana of land in Kathmandu?

Across 572 land listings in Kathmandu district, the median asking price was about Rs 27 lakh per aana. Prime localities such as Baluwatar and Dhumbarahi asked Rs 55 to 60 lakh, while outer areas such as Pharping asked about Rs 7.5 lakh. These are asking prices, which are usually above final sale prices.

How many square feet is one aana?

One aana is 342.25 square feet. There are 16 aana in a ropani (5,476 sq ft), and one aana is divided into 4 paisa.

How do you convert kattha to aana?

One kattha is 3,645 square feet, which is about 10.65 aana. One bigha is 20 kattha (72,900 sq ft).

Are these real sale prices?

No. They are asking prices from public listings on 99aana.com and Hamrobazaar, mostly from 2021, with smaller samples up to 2026. Final sale prices are typically lower.

Is there a free tool to estimate property prices in Nepal?

Yes. The Nepal Property Price Estimator at blog.biggyatkumarpandey.com.np estimates the asking price of a house or land from the locality, area, road access and storeys, using 1,923 real listings.
