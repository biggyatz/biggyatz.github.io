How fast used cars lose value in Nepal

What 422 Hamrobazaar listings, compared with 2026 showroom prices, say about how much value cars keep with age, which brands hold up, and how EVs compare.

Biggyat Kumar Pandey
3 November 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: compared with today's showroom price of the same model, used cars in Nepal ask about 74% at 4–5 years, 60% at 6–8 years, 47% at 9–12 years and 29% at 13+ years. Kilometres barely move the asking price; age and model do.

A used car is the second-biggest purchase many Nepali families make, and there is no Kelley Blue Book for Nepal. Prices get set by word of mouth and by whatever the last listing on Hamrobazaar said. So I collected 422 used-car listings from Hamrobazaar, matched them to 68 make and model families, and compared them with 2026 showroom prices from NepalDrives. The result became a free used-car value estimator. This article covers what the data shows about depreciation.

The typical car in the data is 10 years old, has done 61,500 km and asks Rs 18.6 lakh. Hyundai dominates the market (180 of 422 listings), followed by Suzuki (69), Toyota (29), Nissan (25), Kia (21) and Tata (21).

Petrol and diesel cars. Too few cars under four years old (six listings) to report reliably. Compared with today's new price of the same model, not the price the first owner paid.

Put simply: a five-year-old car asks about three-quarters of a new one, an eight-year-old car about 60%, and by 13 years and older, under a third. Value falls fastest between about 5 and 12 years, then keeps sliding more slowly.

Why so gentle compared with other countries, where cars often lose half their value in five years? Import duties make new cars in Nepal expensive, and those high new-car prices hold up the whole used market.

Brands with at least eight matched listings in this age band. These samples are small, so read them as early signals rather than rankings.

In this data, Nissan and Kia held about 72–73% of the new price over that age range, against about 63% for Hyundai and Suzuki. Because Hyundai and Suzuki are so common, buyers have plenty of choice, which may keep their used prices competitive.

Electric cars

EVs have arrived quickly in Nepal, helped by lower import duties. The sample is still small (17 EV listings from the past eight years matched to a new price), but two- to three-year-old EVs asked about 81% of today's new price (seven listings), and EVs four years and older about 60% (nine listings). The model estimates EVs lose value a little faster with age than petrol cars, which matches what buyers say about battery wear and fast-changing models. I would check this again once there is more data.

Kilometres matter less than you'd think

In the pricing model, which compares cars of the same model and age, doubling the kilometres went with only about 1.4% lower asking prices. Age and model explain most of the price. That is partly because many sellers don't list mileage at all, and partly because buyers in Nepal tend to judge condition in person. Mileage is still worth checking, as a sign of how hard the car was used, but it won't move the asking price much.

How accurate the estimates are

On listings it had not seen during training, the model's typical error is 7.7%, and 85% of estimates land within 20% of the actual asking price. It works from the make and model, year, kilometres and fuel type. It also converts Bikram Sambat years (for example, a "2075 model" is about 2018 AD), which Nepali listings often use.

Tips for buying or selling

  1. Anchor on the new price. Find today's showroom price of the same model, then apply the table above for its age.
  2. Check the model's own history. Some models hold value better than their brand average; the estimator shows a depreciation curve per model.
  3. Treat listings as a ceiling. These are asking prices; most deals close lower.
  4. Inspect for what the data can't see: accident history, service records, tyres and documents (bluebook, tax clearance).

Frequently asked questions

How much value does a car lose per year in Nepal?

From 422 Hamrobazaar listings compared with 2026 showroom prices, a four- to five-year-old car asks about 74% of its model's new price, a six- to eight-year-old about 60%, a nine- to 12-year-old about 47%, and a car 13 years or older about 29%.

Which car brands hold their value best in Nepal?

Among cars aged three to eight years with enough listings, Nissan and Kia held about 72 to 73% of their model's new price, and Hyundai and Suzuki about 63%. The samples are small, so treat this as an early signal.

Do electric cars lose value faster in Nepal?

Two- to three-year-old EVs asked about 81% of today's new price, and EVs four years or older about 60%, in a small sample of 17 listings. The model suggests they lose value slightly faster with age than petrol cars.

How do I convert a Bikram Sambat car model year to AD?

Subtract about 57 years: a 2075 BS model is roughly 2018 AD. The Nepali year starts in mid-April, so the exact AD year can differ by one.

Is there a free tool to check used car prices in Nepal?
